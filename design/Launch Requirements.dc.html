1. What you must have before day one

Five things gate everything else. Nothing below them can be finished until these exist.

#RequirementLead timeWhy it blocks
1A registered legal entity or sole-trader registration1–4 weeksStripe cannot pay out to an unverified individual in most countries, and your privacy policy needs a named data controller.
2A business bank account3–10 daysStripe payouts, domain and hosting subscriptions.
3The domain nameMinutesDNS, SSL, email authentication and Stripe verification all key off it.
4A dedicated admin email address on that domain1 dayEvery account below should be registered to ops@yourdomain, never a personal address.
5A password manager with shared vault1 hourYou are about to create fifteen accounts holding money and user data.

A note on item 4. Registering production infrastructure to a personal Gmail is the single most common early mistake. When ownership transfers, when someone leaves, or when you lose that account, you lose the company. Use a domain mailbox from the start.

3. SSL / TLS certificates

You will not buy a certificate, and you should not want to. Paid SSL is a legacy product; browsers treat a free Let's Encrypt certificate and a €200 certificate identically. What matters is that issuance and renewal are automatic and that nobody else can issue for your domain.

Origin certificateVercel automatic (Let's Encrypt)€0Issued per custom domain when you add it. Renews at 60 days.
Encryption modeCloudflare "Full (strict)"—Anything less allows an unencrypted or unvalidated hop between Cloudflare and your origin. Never use "Flexible".
TLS version floorTLS 1.2 minimum, 1.3 preferred—Set in Cloudflare SSL settings. TLS 1.0 and 1.1 are disallowed by PCI.

5. Database management

Postgres on Neon needs almost no administration, but "almost none" is not "none". These are the standing obligations.

TaskFrequencyWhat it means in practice
Region choiceOnce, irreversiblePick the region closest to your users and keep EU data in the EU. eu-central-1 for a European audience. Changing region later means a dump-and-restore with downtime.
Connection poolingOnceUse Neon's pooled connection string in serverless functions. The direct string is for migrations only. Getting this wrong exhausts connections under modest traffic.
MigrationsEvery releaseForward-only, applied automatically on deploy. Any migration that drops or renames must ship as an expand-and-contract pair over two releases.
Point-in-time recoveryConfigure once7 days on the paid tier. Verify it is actually enabled — the free tier gives 24 hours.
Logical backupNightly, automatedpg_dump to R2 via a cron job, retained 30 days. This protects against provider-level loss, which PITR does not.
Restore drillQuarterlyRestore last night's dump into a scratch branch, run the app against it, record how long it took. A backup never restored is not a backup.
Index reviewQuarterlyCheck pg_stat_statements for the slowest queries. Add indexes for what is actually slow, not what you guessed would be.
Access controlOngoingOne role for the application, one read-only role for debugging, no shared superuser. Nobody connects to production from a laptop.
Anonymised seedMonthlyA scrubbed snapshot for preview branches — emails hashed, measurements randomised, Stripe IDs nulled.

6. Email infrastructure

Email is the requirement people underestimate. If your authentication records are wrong, password resets land in spam and support becomes unmanageable.

RequirementDetail
Sending domainUse a subdomain — mail.yourdomain — so a deliverability problem never poisons your root domain reputation.
SPFOne TXT record, one include per sender. More than ten DNS lookups invalidates the record entirely.
DKIMProvided by Resend as a CNAME or TXT. Must be verified before the first send.
DMARCStart at p=none with a reporting address. Read the reports for two weeks, then move to quarantine, then reject.
Return-path alignmentConfirmed in the Resend dashboard. Misalignment fails DMARC even with valid SPF and DKIM.
Required mailboxescontact@ (public), privacy@ (required by GDPR), ops@ (account registrations), dmarc@ (reports).
UnsubscribeEvery non-transactional email needs a one-click unsubscribe header. Workout reminders count as marketing in the EU unless the user opted in explicitly.
Warm-upSend volume ramps gradually. Do not import a list and send 5,000 emails on day one.

7. Payments — what Stripe will ask for

RequirementDetail
Business verificationRegistration number, address, and the identity document of every beneficial owner. Allow a week; it is occasionally slower.
Bank accountIn the business name, in the country of registration. Personal accounts are rejected for company entities.
Public business detailsA statement descriptor, a support email and a support URL that resolve. Stripe checks these.
Required public pagesTerms of service, privacy policy, refund policy and a contact page must be live before verification. Stripe reviews them.
FeesEuropean cards 1.5% + €0.25; UK 1.5% + £0.20; non-European cards 3.25% + €0.25. Currency conversion adds 2%.
DonationsNot a special product — a one-off Payment Link or Checkout in payment mode. Nothing is tax-deductible for the donor unless you are a registered charity, and you must not imply otherwise.
SubscriptionsStripe Billing plus the hosted Customer Portal. Do not build your own cancellation flow; EU law requires cancellation be as easy as signup, and the portal satisfies that.
TaxStripe Tax, €0 until you have transactions, then a percentage. Digital services VAT in the EU is charged at the customer's rate — this is not optional and not something to hand-roll.
SCAStrong Customer Authentication is mandatory in Europe. Stripe Checkout handles it; a custom card form does not by default.
Webhook endpointA public HTTPS URL with signature verification, live before you take a single payment. Entitlement is granted by webhook, never by a browser redirect.

8. Legal and compliance

These are requirements, not recommendations, for a site that holds health-adjacent data on European users and takes money.

Document or controlRequired byNotes
Privacy policyGDPRMust name the controller, list every processor (Vercel, Neon, Stripe, Resend, Cloudflare, Sentry, Plausible), state retention periods and explain the user's rights.
Terms of serviceStripe, generalIncludes the training disclaimer, account termination terms and limitation of liability.
Cookie / consent noticeePrivacyPlausible is cookieless, so if you avoid third-party embeds you may need only an informational notice rather than a consent gate. Verify with counsel before claiming this.
Medical disclaimerRisk managementVersioned and explicitly accepted at signup with a timestamp — not a footer line. You already have the wording.
Refund policyStripe, EU consumer lawEU digital purchases carry a 14-day withdrawal right unless the customer expressly waives it at checkout.
Data processing registerGDPR Art. 30A spreadsheet listing each processing purpose, lawful basis, data category and retention period. Required even for small operators.
Processor agreementsGDPR Art. 28Every provider above offers a DPA. Accept each one — usually a checkbox in the dashboard — and file the confirmations.
Data export and erasureGDPR Art. 15, 17Self-service in the account settings. Manual handling does not scale and misses the one-month deadline.
Breach procedureGDPR Art. 33A written runbook. You have 72 hours to notify a supervisory authority; that is not time to invent a process.
Accessibility statementEAA (from June 2025)The European Accessibility Act covers e-commerce. A site taking payments needs WCAG 2.2 AA and a published statement.

12. Recommended order of work

WeekFocusOutcome
0Entity, bank, domain, mailbox, password managerYou can legally sign up for everything else.
1Cloudflare DNS, TLS, GitHub, Vercel, NeonA placeholder page serves over HTTPS from your own domain.
2Resend, email authentication, R2, Upstash, SentryA test email passes SPF, DKIM and DMARC.
3Legal pages drafted, DPAs accepted, register startedStripe verification can be submitted.
4Stripe verification, test-mode integrationA test donation completes end to end.
5+Build phases per GW-ARCH-001Infrastructure is no longer on the critical path.

The point of the sequence is that weeks 0 to 4 run in parallel with development and have long external lead times — company registration and Stripe verification wait for other people. Start them first, then build while they process.
